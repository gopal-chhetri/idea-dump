What is Idea Dump?

A full-stack web application that helps software engineers and indie builders decide which project idea to work on next.

The Problem

You have dozens of project ideas — a real-time editor, an AI code review bot, a self-hosted analytics pipeline. But which one aligns best with your current skills? Which is worth the effort? Without data, you pick arbitrarily or get stuck in analysis paralysis.

The Solution

Idea Dump lets you dump every idea freely, then automatically scores each one against your CV profile — your skills, categories, and proficiency weights. The result is a ranked backlog ordered by CV-fit, estimated effort, and novelty. No spreadsheets, no gut-feel.

How it works

From idea dump to prioritised backlog in three steps.

01

Build your CV profile

Add a professional summary and your core skills (languages, frameworks, tools, domain expertise) with proficiency weights (1–5). This calibrates the scoring engine to your unique stack.

02

Dump your ideas

Submit project ideas with title, description, key features, and target use case. Each idea is automatically scored the moment you submit it — no manual review needed.

03

Review your ranked backlog

Your backlog is sorted by computed priority. Pin a favourite, assign a manual rank to override the algorithm, or rescore anytime after updating your CV profile. You stay in control.

How scoring works

Transparent, deterministic, and explainable — no black box.

Fit score (50%)

Tokenizes the idea's title, description, features, and use case, then matches them against your CvSkill.name entries weighted by proficiency. Higher overlap = higher fit.

Effort score (30%)

Derived from feature count and description length as a proxy for scope. Lower effort is rewarded (inverted in the final aggregation) so achievable ideas rise to the top.

Novelty score (20%)

Measures how different an idea is from your existing idea set using text similarity. Diverse ideas score higher, preventing a backlog full of variations on the same theme.

Final score = 0.5 × fit + 0.3 × (100 − effort) + 0.2 × novelty  ·  Weights are configurable via environment variables.
